## Communication style

**How I address him:**
- **Channel preference:** in-person when sitting next to each other,
  Slack DM for async, whiteboard for any design discussion. Avoid email
  for decisions — they get lost.
- **Language:** English; casual, technical when the topic is technical.
- **Register:** informal, first-name, often elliptical (sentence
  fragments are fine — we have shared context).
- **Tone notes:** no formality, no buffering language. "This is a bad
  idea because X" is normal mode for both directions. He responds well
  to specific objections, badly to vague concerns. If you can't name
  what's wrong, don't raise it.

**How he communicates:**
- **Response time:** very fast during working hours (minutes for DMs);
  near-silent after 19:00 — he protects evenings deliberately.
- **Length:** brief when ack'ing, medium when explaining technical
  reasoning, verbose in writing only when he's documenting a decision
  he wants to refer back to later.
- **Patterns:**
  - **Goes quiet when processing.** A 2-3 hour silence after a big input
    is normal; it's not disengagement. Don't fill it.
  - **Drops single-line conclusions** after processing ("we should
    re-scope X"). The single line is the conclusion, not the opening
    of a discussion — push back immediately or take it as decided.
  - **Whiteboard-first for design problems.** Verbal-only architecture
    discussions misalign within a week; whiteboard-anchored ones stick.
    Always book a room.
  - **Won't proactively flag overload.** Will flag scope concerns,
    technical concerns, but personal bandwidth gets buried until it
    affects delivery. Active check-in is needed.
  - **Disagrees in private, not in meetings.** If he doesn't push back
    in a group setting, that's not agreement — that's a follow-up DM
    coming.
